Citation discipline (13 points):

2. Use only papers in the 77-PDF corpus. Every bibkey cited must exist literally in NB1_CQTD_CORE.bib. Do not infer bibkeys from titles, folder names, examples, or model memory. If a bibkey cannot be verified, do not cite the paper.
3. Cite by exact bibkey from NB1_CQTD_CORE.bib. The References section must use the official title from the .bib file, not paraphrased.
4. One citation per claim per paper.
5. Single numbering system. Inline [N] equals References entry N. Ordered by first appearance.
6. References section lists ONLY papers cited inline.
7. Citation-from-citation safeguard.
8. Reference-number-extraction safeguard.
9. Subject-propagation safeguard.

11. "No evidence found in uploaded sources" rather than fabricate.
12. When listing papers in the References section, use the title format from the NB1_CQTD_CORE.csv file (clean text, no curly brackets around capitalised words). The .bib file's title field surrounds capitalised words with curly brackets (e.g., "Program {Code} {Generation} with {Generative} {AIs}") because BibTeX requires those for case preservation, but the curly brackets are formatting instructions for LaTeX compilation, not part of the actual title. Strip them. The expected format is: "Program Code Generation with Generative AIs", not "Program {Code} {Generation} with {Generative} {AIs}
13. British English throughout.

Step 1: Construct a source-control table of every construct-paper-finding relationship before drafting prose. Verify all bibkeys.

Step 3: Generate References AFTER prose is stable, ordered by first appearance.

Task: Phase 4.5 — Research Gaps Taxonomy
Produce a DOCX document that catalogues and classifies the research gaps revealed by the corpus, structured as a taxonomy across multiple gap types (methodological, empirical, conceptual, contextual). Each gap is articulated with specificity about what future research would resolve it.
WHAT TO PRODUCE:
1. Overview — 2 paragraphs framing the analytical purpose: a corpus of 77 papers reveals what has been studied, but also what has not been adequately studied. This artefact classifies those gaps and translates them into actionable research questions for future work.
2. Gaps organised by category — Four gap categories, each with multiple specific gaps:
2.1 Methodological gaps — 4-6 specific gaps. For each, structured as:
- 2.1.X.1 Gap statement (one sentence)
- 2.1.X.2 Why this is a gap: explain what evidence is missing or what study design has not been applied
- 2.1.X.3 Evidence from the corpus: cite 3-6 papers that either expose the gap or attempt to close it but at insufficient scale
- 2.1.X.4 What would resolve it: name specific study designs, sample sizes, or contexts needed
- 2.1.X.5 Priority: high / medium / low for the field's progression
Examples of methodological gaps to consider:
- Longitudinal multi-organisation studies of AI-assisted teams
- Randomised controlled trials at scale with diverse expertise levels
- Post-deployment monitoring of AI-generated code over multi-year windows
- Standardised real-world (non-benchmark) evaluation protocols
- Cross-cultural and cross-organisational comparative studies
2.2 Empirical gaps — 4-6 specific gaps with same structure.
Examples to consider:
- Multi-language and multi-domain coverage (most corpus is Python-centric)
- Underrepresented developer populations (women, students from non-elite institutions, non-Western teams)
- Quality effects on safety-critical and high-stakes domains
- Maintenance effort attribution over 12+ month horizons
- Cost-benefit analysis with reliable hidden-cost accounting
2.3 Conceptual gaps — 3-5 specific gaps with same structure.
Examples to consider:
- Operationalisation of "trust" in human-AI development contexts
- Operationalisation of "interaction quality" beyond prompt formulation
- Distinction between AI-introduced and AI-amplified technical debt
- Theoretical models of developer-AI cognitive co-construction
- Definition and measurement of prompting and validation literacy as competencies
2.4 Contextual gaps — 3-4 specific gaps with same structure.
Examples to consider:
- Open-source vs proprietary codebase dynamics
- Small organisation vs large organisation deployment patterns
- Education and onboarding contexts beyond university CS programmes
- Regulated industry contexts (healthcare, finance, safety-critical)
3. Gap interaction analysis — 2-3 paragraphs identifying gaps that compound each other. For example: the empirical gap on longitudinal effects compounded by the methodological gap on multi-organisation studies creates a particularly acute knowledge deficit about long-term enterprise AI-assisted development outcomes.
4. Priority gaps for the next research cycle — A closing assessment (2-3 paragraphs) naming the 5-7 gaps the field most urgently needs to address, with brief justification.
5. Headline gaps table — Closing table with 8-12 rows of the highest-priority gaps. Columns: # | Gap | Category | Why priority | Required study type.
6. References — Ordered by first appearance.
7. Reference-alignment spot-check.
Additional constraints:
- Each gap must be supported by corpus evidence (i.e., papers that either expose the gap or insufficiently address it). Do not list gaps as abstract concerns without empirical traction in the corpus.
- Distinguish "gap because no one has studied this" from "gap because studies disagree and need replication". Both are legitimate but conceptually distinct.
