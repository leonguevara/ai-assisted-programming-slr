3. Cite by exact bibkey from NB1_CQTD_CORE.bib. The References section must use the official title from the .bib file, not paraphrased.
4. One citation per claim per paper.
5. Single numbering system. Inline [N] equals References entry N. Ordered by first appearance.
6. References section lists ONLY papers cited inline.
7. Citation-from-citation safeguard.
8. Reference-number-extraction safeguard.
9. Subject-propagation safeguard.

11. "No evidence found in uploaded sources" rather than fabricate.
12. British English throughout.

Workflow discipline:
Apply "evidence matrix first, prose second, references third, validation last":

Step 1: Construct a source-control table of every construct-paper-finding relationship before drafting prose. Verify all bibkeys.
Step 2: Draft each item using only papers from that table.
Step 3: Generate References AFTER prose is stable, ordered by first appearance.
Step 4: Run self-validation: zero ghosts, no orphan commas, sub-points numbered, no paper in >5 items, spot-check 3 citations, no fabricated bibkeys.

Task: Phase 4.4 — Emerging Themes Articulation

Produce a DOCX document that identifies and articulates research themes emerging in the most recent portion of the corpus (papers from 2024 onwards), particularly themes that have not yet stabilised into consensus but signal future research directions.

This artefact is intended as PROSE READY FOR THE MANUSCRIPT, suitable for the Discussion or Future Work section. Use clear academic prose.
WHAT TO PRODUCE:

1. Overview — 2 paragraphs framing the analytical purpose: among the 77 corpus papers, those published since 2024 reveal research themes that are not yet captured in the established consensus. These themes signal the field's direction over the next 2-3 years.

2. Emerging themes — 6-9 themes, each as a substantive subsection (1-2 paragraphs of prose each). For each theme:
2.X [Theme name] — Substantive paragraph(s) covering:
- The theme statement (what is being claimed or investigated)
- Why it is emerging now (technical capability shift, empirical maturity, recognition of prior gap)
- Representative papers (3-6 papers from 2024-2026 with bibkeys)
- Quantitative anchors when available (apply subject-propagation safeguard)
- Open questions the theme raises
Suggested emerging themes to explore (adjust based on corpus):
- Agentic autonomy and safety boundaries
- Repository-level long-term technical debt accumulation
- Reproducibility and dependency ecosystem fragility
- Prompt engineering as an emerging professional skill
- AI-generated code maintenance burden over time
- Multi-model interaction dynamics (planner-executor, tool-using agents)
- Verification literacy as a developer competency
- Industrial deployment patterns and organisational adaptation
- Skill amplification vs deskilling longitudinal effects
Drop themes the corpus does not actually support with 3+ post-2023 papers.

3. Meta-observations on emerging themes — 2-3 paragraphs reflecting on what these themes collectively reveal about the field's direction. Are they primarily technical (about better tools) or socio-technical (about human-AI co-development practices)? What does this say about the maturity of the research area?

4. Tensions among emerging themes — A short section (2-3 paragraphs) identifying where emerging themes pull in different directions. For example, the push toward greater agentic autonomy vs the parallel emergence of validation-literacy frameworks. These tensions are productive for the Discussion section.

5. Manuscript placement implications — 1-2 paragraphs on how these emerging themes should be deployed in the manuscript. Specifically: which themes belong in Discussion Section 5 (interpretation), which in Future Work, and which deserve brief mention in the Introduction as motivation.

6. References — Ordered by first appearance.

7. Reference-alignment spot-check.

Additional constraints:
- Write in flowing academic prose for direct manuscript adaptation.
- A theme qualifies as "emerging" if 60%+ of supporting papers are from 2024 or later.
- Be careful not to confuse "emerging" with "trendy" — the criterion is empirical concentration in recent years, not popularity.
