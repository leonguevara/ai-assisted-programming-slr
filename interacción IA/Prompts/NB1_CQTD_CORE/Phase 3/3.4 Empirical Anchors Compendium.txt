CONSOLIDATED CONSTRAINTS — apply ALL from the first draft, not retroactively.

Citation discipline (12 points):
1. Breadth across all 77 papers. Every paper in the corpus is a valid source, including Bayram, Belozerov, and Siroš, but no paper has privileged status. When multiple papers support a claim, cite the broader set rather than defaulting to the same few.
2. Use only papers in the 77-PDF corpus. Every bibkey cited must exist literally in NB1_CQTD_CORE.bib. Do not infer bibkeys from titles, folder names, examples in the prompt, or model memory. If a bibkey cannot be verified, do not cite the paper — even if it would round out a quota.
3. Cite by exact bibkey from NB1_CQTD_CORE.bib. The References section must use the official title from the .bib file, not a paraphrased version.
4. One citation per claim per paper. Do not cite the same paper twice (once to abstract, once to body) for the same finding.
5. Single numbering system across the document. Inline [N] equals References entry N. References are ordered by first appearance in section 2, not alphabetically.
6. The References section lists ONLY the papers actually cited inline, not the full corpus.
7. Citation-from-citation safeguard: if a figure or finding is reported by a citation within a paper (e.g., "Pearce et al. report 40%"), do not attribute that figure to the citing paper. Only count what the paper itself empirically produced.
8. Reference-number-extraction safeguard: do not treat bracketed reference markers (e.g., "[21, 41, 49]"), arXiv identifiers (e.g., "2107.03374"), or bibliography indices as quantitative findings.
9. Subject-propagation safeguard: when a sentence contains multiple figures governed by a single subject (e.g., "GPT-5 achieves 98.4%, 82.6%, and 26.9%"), propagate the subject to every derived figure or row. Do not record "not specified" when the source sentence names a specific model.
10. No paper anchoring more than 5 claims/items unless the artefact explicitly allows it.
11. If you cannot find evidence in the uploaded PDFs for a particular claim, say "No evidence found in uploaded sources" rather than inventing one. No hallucinations.
12. British English throughout.

Workflow discipline:
Apply the "evidence matrix first, prose second, references third, validation last" workflow established in the Phase 3.1 Lessons Learned:

Step 1: Construct a source-control table (mental or actual) of every paper-item relationship before drafting prose. Verify all bibkeys against NB1_CQTD_CORE.bib at this stage.
Step 2: Draft the prose of each item using only papers from the source-control table. Do not introduce new papers during drafting.
Step 3: Generate the References section AFTER the prose is stable, ordered by first appearance.
Step 4: Run a self-validation pass before delivery:
- Every inline [N] has a corresponding References entry.
- No empty brackets, orphan commas, or formatting residue.
- Sub-points are numbered correctly.
- No paper appears in more than 5 items.
- Spot-check 3 inline citations against References.
- No fabricated bibkeys.

Regeneration over modification: If on review the draft has substantive issues, regenerate the whole document rather than apply local patches. Iterative editing of complex internally-linked documents introduces ghost citations, orphan commas, and semantic drift (the "iterative-editing entropy" pattern). Regenerate from scratch when significant changes are needed.

Validity over breadth: A claim with 6 verified papers is preferable to a claim with 12 papers if 6 of them are weak, irrelevant, or unverified. Quality outweighs volume.

DOCX structure:

The Phase 3.4 Anchors Compendium has the correct structure (67 anchors, 10 themes balanced, 54 papers, frequency caps respected, 4 sheets in XLSX, anchors numbered 2.X.2.Y in DOCX), but the content is critically insufficient for manuscript use. Three problems must be corrected:

Problem 1 (most critical): Anchors have no description of what they measure.

Every anchor in the DOCX is currently structured as "[FIGURE] — [paper title] (Author, bibkey) [N]. Study design: X. Defensibility: Y. Suggested use: Z."

This is unusable for the manuscript because a reader cannot tell what the figure represents. What does "25%" mean? Is it accuracy? Defect rate? Pass rate on HumanEval? Vulnerability rate?

Each anchor must be re-narrated to include a measurement description. The required format is:

"2.1.2.1 25% [accuracy on HumanEval Python problems, ChatGPT model evaluated on 164 problems] — Comparative Analysis of AI Models for Python Code Generation: A HumanEval Benchmark Study (Bayram, bayram_comparative_2025) [1]. ..."

Also, you are using boilerplates for Study design and Defensibility.  Each anchor should have its own narrative (see problem 3 below).

Equivalently in the XLSX, the columns must be populated so the reader can construct this sentence for this specific example:

- 'Exact figure' = "25%"
