Continuing Phase 3 of the SLR section on Code Quality and Technical Debt in AI-Assisted Programming. Same NB1_CQTD_CORE corpus (77 PDFs in the Google Drive folder), same Phase 2 extractive workbooks (11,628 atomic findings across 2.1-2.6).

CONSOLIDATED CONSTRAINTS — apply ALL from the first draft, not retroactively.

Citation discipline (12 points):
1. Breadth across all 77 papers. Every paper in the corpus is a valid source, including Bayram, Belozerov, and Siroš, but no paper has privileged status. When multiple papers support a claim, cite the broader set rather than defaulting to the same few.
2. Use only papers in the 77-PDF corpus. Every bibkey cited must exist literally in NB1_CQTD_CORE.bib. Do not infer bibkeys from titles, folder names, examples in the prompt, or model memory. If a bibkey cannot be verified, do not cite the paper — even if it would round out a quota.
3. Cite by exact bibkey from NB1_CQTD_CORE.bib. The References section must use the official title from the .bib file, not a paraphrased version.
4. One citation per claim per paper. Do not cite the same paper twice (once to abstract, once to body) for the same finding.
5. Single numbering system across the document. Inline [N] equals References entry N. References are ordered by first appearance in section 2, not alphabetically.
6. The References section lists ONLY the papers actually cited inline, not the full corpus.
7. Citation-from-citation safeguard: if a figure or finding is reported by a citation within a paper (e.g., "Pearce et al. report 40%"), do not attribute that figure to the citing paper. Only count what the paper itself empirically produced.
8. Reference-number-extraction safeguard: do not treat bracketed reference markers (e.g., "[21, 41, 49]"), arXiv identifiers (e.g., "2107.03374"), or bibliography indices as quantitative findings.
9. Subject-propagation safeguard: when a sentence contains multiple figures governed by a single subject (e.g., "GPT-5 achieves 98.4%, 82.6%, and 26.9%"), propagate the subject to every derived figure or row. Do not record "not specified" when the source sentence names a specific model.
10. No paper anchoring more than 5 claims/items unless the artefact explicitly allows it.
11. If you cannot find evidence in the uploaded PDFs for a particular claim, say "No evidence found in uploaded sources" rather than inventing one. No hallucinations.
12. British English throughout.

Workflow discipline:
Apply the "evidence matrix first, prose second, references third, validation last" workflow established in the Phase 3.1 Lessons Learned:

Step 1: Construct a source-control table (mental or actual) of every paper-item relationship before drafting prose. Verify all bibkeys against NB1_CQTD_CORE.bib at this stage.
Step 2: Draft the prose of each item using only papers from the source-control table. Do not introduce new papers during drafting.
Step 3: Generate the References section AFTER the prose is stable, ordered by first appearance.
Step 4: Run a self-validation pass before delivery:
- Every inline [N] has a corresponding References entry.
- No empty brackets, orphan commas, or formatting residue.
- Sub-points are numbered correctly.
- No paper appears in more than 5 items.
- Spot-check 3 inline citations against References.
- No fabricated bibkeys.

Regeneration over modification: If on review the draft has substantive issues, regenerate the whole document rather than apply local patches. Iterative editing of complex internally-linked documents introduces ghost citations, orphan commas, and semantic drift (the "iterative-editing entropy" pattern). Regenerate from scratch when significant changes are needed.

Validity over breadth: A claim with 6 verified papers is preferable to a claim with 12 papers if 6 of them are weak, irrelevant, or unverified. Quality outweighs volume.

Task: Phase 3.3 — Methodological Pattern Atlas
Produce a methodological pattern atlas: a DOCX document that maps which study designs in the corpus yield which kinds of findings.
Use as input: the Phase 1.2 (Methodology Distribution Table) and Phase 1.4 (Quality Operationalisation) workbooks, supplemented by the six Phase 2 workbooks.

WHAT TO PRODUCE:
1. Overview — 2 paragraphs framing why methodological pattern matters. The reader should be able to assess whether a particular finding is artefactual of the study design or robust across designs.

2. Study design categories — Establish 6-8 design categories based on what the corpus actually contains (verify via Phase 1.2). For each category, exactly 8 numbered sub-points:
- 2.X.1 Category name and one-sentence definition.
- 2.X.2 Number of papers in the corpus using this design (verify from Phase 1.2).
- 2.X.3 Typical sample size range and scale (verify from Phase 1.2 sample-size column).
- 2.X.4 Typical evaluation contexts: benchmarks, datasets, real-world settings.
- 2.X.5 Strengths: what this design measures well (1-2 sentences).
- 2.X.6 Limitations: what this design systematically misses or biases (1-2 sentences).
- 2.X.7 Representative papers: 5-8 papers per category with bibkeys, drawn from the verified Phase 1.2 list.
- 2.X.8 Typical findings produced by this design, with 2-3 quantitative anchors attributed to specific papers (apply subject-propagation rule).

Suggested categories (refine based on Phase 1.2):
- (a) Benchmark-based comparative evaluations (HumanEval, MBPP, SWE-bench, etc.)
- (b) Repository-mining studies at scale
- (c) User studies with controlled groups and timing measurement
- (d) Observational studies in industrial/workplace settings
- (e) Security-focused empirical assessments (CWE-based, static-analysis-based)
- (f) Agentic / multi-step workflow evaluations
- (g) Qualitative interview-based or diary studies
- (h) Reproducibility and dependency studies

3. Cross-design pattern analysis — 3-4 paragraphs identifying which findings appear in multiple design categories (high robustness) versus which findings appear in only one design (lower robustness).
- Findings that converge across designs: list 5-8 robust empirical claims with their cross-design support (cite which papers in which categories).
- Findings observable only in one design: list 4-6 claims that depend on a specific methodology, and explain what this implies for interpretation.

Papers cited in this section must come from the categories established in section 2.

4. Methodological gaps — 2-3 paragraphs identifying study designs that are systematically underrepresented in the corpus. Examples might be: longitudinal studies, randomised controlled trials at scale, multi-organisation studies, post-deployment monitoring studies. If a proposed gap actually has papers in the corpus, do not call it a gap. Cite the gap-claim with reference to which Phase 1.2 categories are sparse.

5. Implications for the manuscript — A short section advising how the SLR should weight findings by methodological strength. Claims supported by 8 papers across 4 different designs should be presented as robust; claims supported by 3 papers from one design type should be presented as suggestive.

6. Headline design-pattern anchors — A closing table of 10 entries. Columns: # | Finding category | Design that produced it | Representative paper | Quantitative anchor.

7. References — Generated AFTER prose is stable. Ordered by first appearance.

8. Reference-alignment spot-check — Verify 3 inline citations.

Additional task-specific constraints:
- Each design category must cite at least 5 distinct papers.
- If the corpus does not contain a proposed design category (e.g., no longitudinal studies), note it as a gap in section 4 rather than inventing papers to fill the category.
- The category counts in 2.X.2 must match the actual count in Phase 1.2 Methodology Distribution Table.
