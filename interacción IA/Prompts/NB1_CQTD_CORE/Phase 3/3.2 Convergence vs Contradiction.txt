Continuing Phase 3 of the SLR on Code Quality and Technical Debt in AI-Assisted Programming. Same NB1_CQTD_CORE corpus (77 PDFs in the Google Drive folder), same Phase 2 extractive workbooks (11,628 atomic findings across 2.1-2.6).

CONSOLIDATED CONSTRAINTS — apply ALL from the first draft, not retroactively.

Citation discipline (12 points):
1. Breadth across all 77 papers. Every paper in the corpus is a valid source, including Bayram, Belozerov, and Siroš, but no paper has privileged status. When multiple papers support a claim, cite the broader set rather than defaulting to the same few.
2. Use only papers in the 77-PDF corpus. Every bibkey cited must exist literally in NB1_CQTD_CORE.bib. Do not infer bibkeys from titles, folder names, examples in the prompt, or model memory. If a bibkey cannot be verified, do not cite the paper — even if it would round out a quota.
3. Cite by exact bibkey from NB1_CQTD_CORE.bib. The References section must use the official title from the .bib file, not a paraphrased version.
4. One citation per claim per paper. Do not cite the same paper twice (once to abstract, once to body) for the same finding.
5. Single numbering system across the document. Inline [N] equals References entry N. References are ordered by first appearance in section 2, not alphabetically.
6. The References section lists ONLY the papers actually cited inline, not the full corpus.
7. Citation-from-citation safeguard: if a figure or finding is reported by a citation within a paper (e.g., "Pearce et al. report 40%"), do not attribute that figure to the citing paper. Only count what the paper itself empirically produced.
8. Reference-number-extraction safeguard: do not treat bracketed reference markers (e.g., "[21, 41, 49]"), arXiv identifiers (e.g., "2107.03374"), or bibliography indices as quantitative findings.
9. Subject-propagation safeguard: when a sentence contains multiple figures governed by a single subject (e.g., "GPT-5 achieves 98.4%, 82.6%, and 26.9%"), propagate the subject to every derived figure or row. Do not record "not specified" when the source sentence names a specific model.
10. No paper anchoring more than 5 claims/items unless the artefact explicitly allows it.
11. If you cannot find evidence in the uploaded PDFs for a particular claim, say "No evidence found in uploaded sources" rather than inventing one. No hallucinations.
12. British English throughout.

Workflow discipline:
Apply the "evidence matrix first, prose second, references third, validation last" workflow established in the Phase 3.1 Lessons Learned:

Step 1: Construct a source-control table (mental or actual) of every paper-item relationship before drafting prose. Verify all bibkeys against NB1_CQTD_CORE.bib at this stage.
Step 2: Draft the prose of each item using only papers from the source-control table. Do not introduce new papers during drafting.
Step 3: Generate the References section AFTER the prose is stable, ordered by first appearance.
Step 4: Run a self-validation pass before delivery:
- Every inline [N] has a corresponding References entry.
- No empty brackets, orphan commas, or formatting residue.
- Sub-points are numbered correctly.
- No paper appears in more than 5 items.
- Spot-check 3 inline citations against References.
- No fabricated bibkeys.

Regeneration over modification: If on review the draft has substantive issues, regenerate the whole document rather than apply local patches. Iterative editing of complex internally-linked documents introduces ghost citations, orphan commas, and semantic drift (the "iterative-editing entropy" pattern). Regenerate from scratch when significant changes are needed.

Validity over breadth: A claim with 6 verified papers is preferable to a claim with 12 papers if 6 of them are weak, irrelevant, or unverified. Quality outweighs volume.

Task: Phase 3.2 — Contradiction Map
Produce a corpus-wide contradiction map: a DOCX document that identifies the empirical claims where the 77 papers disagree, with explicit partition of which papers support each side.

WHAT TO PRODUCE:

1. Overview — 2 paragraphs framing the three kinds of disagreement:
(a) Genuine contradiction: papers measuring the same construct under comparable conditions arrive at incompatible conclusions.
(b) Context effect: papers arrive at different conclusions because they study different contexts; the disagreement is apparent, not genuine.
(c) Unresolved tension: a question the corpus poses but does not yet answer with sufficient evidence.

2. Contradictions and tensions — 8-12 entries grouped by theme. For each entry, exactly 7 numbered sub-points:
- 2.X.1 The empirical question being contested (one sentence). Inline citations to the papers on both sides, distributed.
- 2.X.2 Kind of disagreement: genuine contradiction / context effect / unresolved tension.
- 2.X.3 Side A — what some papers find. Cite 4-8 papers with bibkeys. Include 2-4 quantitative anchors attributed to specific papers (subject-propagation rule).
- 2.X.4 Side B — what other papers find. Cite 4-8 papers with bibkeys. Include 2-4 quantitative anchors.
- 2.X.5 If applicable, Side C — intermediate or moderating view. Cite 2-4 papers.
- 2.X.6 Best interpretation: 2-3 sentences arguing how the disagreement should be read. If context effect, name the context variable (task type, developer expertise, model version, benchmark vs real-world, single-shot vs multi-turn, etc.). If genuine contradiction, name the methodological difference that might explain it. If unresolved, name what evidence would resolve it. The papers named narratively in 2.X.6 must come from 2.X.3, 2.X.4, or 2.X.5 — do not introduce new papers.
- 2.X.7 Implication for the manuscript: 1 sentence on how this disagreement should be presented in the SLR narrative (e.g., "treat as context effect", "flag as open question", "report both sides and note the unresolved status").

Suggested questions to explore (adjust based on what the corpus actually supports — do not force a question if the evidence is one-sided):
- Q1: Do frontier LLMs match human developer performance on real-world tasks?
- Q2: Does prompt engineering substantially reduce defect rates?
- Q3: Are agentic systems safer or less safe than single-shot generation?
- Q4: Do less experienced developers benefit more from AI tools?
- Q5: Does Chain-of-Thought reasoning improve code correctness?
- Q6: Are repository-level security risks higher with AI vs without?
- Q7: Does multi-turn interaction increase or decrease code quality?
- Q8: Is AI-generated technical debt comparable to human-generated debt or systematically different?
Drop any question if the corpus does not support a genuine two-sided disagreement on it.

3. Pattern observations — 2-3 paragraphs reflecting on what kinds of disagreements predominate: are most contradictions actually context effects? Are some research questions genuinely unresolved? Is methodological heterogeneity the main driver?

4. Headline tensions — A closing table of the 8-10 most important tensions/contradictions. Columns: # | Tension | Side A papers | Side B papers | Interpretation | Strength of disagreement.

5. References — Generated AFTER the prose is stable. Ordered by first appearance in section 2. Numbered sequentially without gaps.

6. Reference-alignment spot-check — Verify 3 inline citations against the References list, using the final numbering (not stale numbering from a draft).

Additional task-specific constraints:
- Distinguish "papers disagree" from "evidence is mixed in one paper" — these are different epistemic states.
- Always cite the papers explicitly for each side. Do not say "some studies vs others"; say "[N1][N2][N3] vs [N4][N5][N6]".
- For each contradiction, the papers in Side A and Side B must come from different sets — no paper should be on both sides of the same disagreement.
- If the corpus does not actually support a proposed contradiction, drop it. Do not invent contradiction where the corpus is unanimous.
